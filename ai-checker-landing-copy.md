## 4. Body copy

**See which parts of your draft read generic or machine-like, and why**

Paste a draft and the checker reads it the way a careful writing coach would. Each of seven writing signals gets a 0–100 signal-strength score: how strongly that pattern shows up in your text. It is not the chance that AI wrote it. There is no pass or fail grade and no authorship verdict. Then the checker shows the exact lines it noticed and asks you a question about each one.

## What the checker looks at

Seven signals, all on a public rubric with a version number (v1.0), so you can see how every score was reached:

- **Sentence-length variance:** do your sentences all run about the same length?
- **Burstiness:** do short and long stretches alternate the way they do in most people's writing?
- **Vocabulary vs. your own writing:** do the words match how you usually write? This one needs your voice check first.
- **Tone consistency:** does the voice stay steady, or shift between paragraphs?
- **Topic sentences:** does each paragraph open with a clear point you would recognize as yours?
- **Personal specifics:** are there details only you would know, like a class discussion, a place or a number you collected?
- **Stock phrases:** are there stand-by openers and wrap-ups that could fit any paper?

## What you get back

You'll see a preview right away: the overall result plus your top two signals. Enter your email for the full report, which adds all seven signals, a section-by-section view and a copy in your inbox. You'll confirm you're 13 or older. Checking a draft needs no account.

The overall result is one of three labels: "Reads like you," "A few generic spots" or "Several machine-like patterns." Each flagged item quotes a named tell from your own text and pairs it with a coaching question. For example:

- A run of five sentences between 17 and 19 words. *Which of these would you actually say out loud?*
- A closing line like "In conclusion, it is important to note that X plays a vital role in today's fast-paced world." (This is an example. Your report quotes your own closing line.) *What's the one point this paragraph is making, in your words?*
- A paragraph that names no person, place, number or first-hand moment. *What did you actually see in that source?*
- Words that weren't in your sample, such as "multifaceted, meticulous, landscape," once you have a voice check. *Would you say this out loud?*

Plenty of drafts come back "reads fine," and we say so plainly. A low score is a good outcome: it means we did not find much of that pattern.

## What no detector can tell you

No tool, including this one or Turnitin or GPTZero, can prove whether AI wrote something. These results are style signals, not a verdict. False positives are real, especially for ESL writers and very formal writers.

In a 2023 study in *Patterns*, Liang and colleagues ran seven widely used GPT detectors on 91 TOEFL essays written by non-native English speakers. On average, the detectors labeled 61.3% of those human-written essays as AI-generated. The same detectors classified U.S. eighth-grade essays much more accurately. Turnitin itself tells instructors to use its indicator to start a conversation, not to draw a conclusion, and in 2023 reported a sentence-level false-positive rate of about 4%.

That is why we score patterns instead of issuing a verdict, and why we ask questions instead of making accusations. Use the results to look harder at your own draft. We don't claim an accuracy rate for this tool.

## Does this sound like you?

That is the only question the checker is built to help with. If a section scores high on stock phrases, the fix is not to shuffle words. It is to put your own thinking back in: your example, your reason, your sentence. Writing you can explain is writing you can defend.

## 5. FAQ (7 questions, in sync with the JSON-LD)

**1. Can this tell me whether my paper will be flagged as AI?**
No. Nobody can promise how another detector or a professor will read your paper, and the scores here are not a chance of being flagged. The checker scores seven writing signals for how strongly each pattern shows up, quotes the lines behind them, and asks questions so you can judge whether the draft sounds like you.

**2. Is this an AI detector?**
It is a coaching tool for people searching for an AI detector for student papers. It rates writing patterns. It does not prove who wrote something, it does not give a pass or fail grade, and it does not estimate the probability that AI wrote your text.

**3. What are the seven signals?**
Sentence-length variance, burstiness, vocabulary compared with your own writing, tone consistency, topic sentences, personal specifics, and stock phrases. Each signal gets a 0–100 signal-strength score, and the full report adds a score for each section of your draft. The vocabulary signal needs a voice check first, because it compares your draft with your own writing.

**4. What do the scores and labels mean?**
A signal-strength score from 0 to 100 shows how strongly a writing pattern appears in your text. It is not the chance that AI wrote it. Each signal also reads low, moderate or high. The overall result is one of three labels: 'Reads like you' (0–39), 'A few generic spots' (40–69) or 'Several machine-like patterns' (70–100). A low score is a good outcome, because it means we did not find much of that pattern. Many drafts come back 'reads fine.'

**5. Can a checker like this be wrong about my writing?**
Yes. No tool, including this one, Turnitin or GPTZero, can prove whether AI wrote something. False positives are real, especially for ESL writers and very formal writers. In a 2023 study in Patterns, Liang and colleagues found seven widely used GPT detectors labeled 61.3% of 91 human-written TOEFL essays as AI-generated on average. Treat every score here as a conversation starter, never as proof.

**6. Do I need an account, and what is the voice check?**
You do not need an account or a card to check a draft. You see a free preview right away, and your email unlocks the full report. The optional one-minute voice check is typed, not recorded. It builds a baseline of your own vocabulary, tone and scholarly register so the checker can compare your draft with your real writing. The voice check creates a free account, and it leads into your free first paper, with no card required.

**7. What happens to the draft I paste?**
Drafts aren't stored unless you are signed in and opt in. We never use drafts for training. You can check a draft with no account. If you enter your email for the full report, we keep the scores and the short quoted lines in a private link for 30 days so you can reopen it, then we delete them. We do not keep the full text unless you have an account and ask us to save it. You must be 13 or older to use the tool.
