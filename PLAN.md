## North Star: the coaching loop (Colton, Nov 24 12:04 AM)
Pau is smart, not a kind reader: if we know something, share it; if we don't, figure it out, and sometimes the investigation takes asking. Every paper runs the same six-step loop:
1. **Clarify**: a plain read-back (what's asked, what a strong answer must include, how it's graded). The student confirms with the strict yes.
2. **Material catch-up**: a short, jargon-free summary of the course material per the source policy (uploads first; then her own labeled knowledge of a named well-known work; then a found legitimate source; otherwise an honest ask for photos, a paste, a PDF or a link). She offers to go deeper, say it simpler, or find a real link-checked video.
3. **Direction and decision**: 2–3 real, named options; the student picks one and says why (saved verbatim); personal experience first when it fits.
4. **Idea extraction**, mapped to sections.
5. **Guided drafting**, section by section, with a logic check after each (never a hard stop).
6. **Tighten and deliver.**
Today (Colton t221u, Nov 25, 2026; PC-32) Stage 1 covers step 1 plus the start of step 3 in a short form: the assignment and any material in, the plain read-back confirmed, and one light "have you thought about what to write?" (an idea is reflected back, confirmed and saved; none is fine). The material catch-up (step 2) happens on request and in Stage 2; the decision with its reason (step 3) moved to Stage 2's stance interview. The shared paper memory (paper_context: read-back, summaries, decision with reasons) carries into later stages for text and the Grok Voice relay (get_paper_context).

## 1. Product Goal
Help students write better papers by coaching them through a structured process so they learn and retain the material. The AI never writes the paper for them.

## 2. Core Non-Negotiables
- Student owns every sentence.
- One frame / one question at a time.
- Teach-then-ask on every concept.
- Assignment upload and parsing happens before any coaching questions.
- Progress saves on every submission and on pause. Signed-in users can leave and resume.
- Logic checks give feedback or strong warnings, never hard stops.
- Visual design and copy must feel calm, clear, and student-friendly (not generic SaaS).

## 3. Pages & Primary Job of Each
- **Homepage** — Explain the six-stage coaching path and the "we never write for you" promise. Clear "Start free paper" CTA.
- **Pricing** — $0 first paper (no card), $9 single, $15 for 3, optional $1.99 audio. Emphasize no subscription.
- **Auth (Sign up / Log in)** — Simple email + password. Clear "Continue in demo" path that warns progress may be lost.
- **Papers Dashboard** — List of papers with resume point for each.
- **Stage 1 – Intake** — Upload assignment (photos, PDFs, text). System extracts prompt, format, rubric, sections. Student confirms. Then, short and sweet (Colton t221u/t222u, Nov 25, 2026): a conversational summary of what the paper is truly asking (the read-back; they confirm), then one light question: have they already thought about what to write? An idea is acknowledged, summarized back for confirmation and saved (at most one light clarifying question; never an interview); no idea is fine. Where they start (has ideas / has read the material / no clue) is saved for Stage 2. Then Stage 2. No voice-sample step, no style/leader pick, no story angle, no early take, no spark offer in Stage 1 (see §9).
- **Stage 2 – Stance / Research Loop** — Section-by-section: summarize what the section needs → probe what the student already knows or wants to explore → offer 3–5 research leads (one wildcard) → student confirms ideas. Logic checkpoint after each section.
- **Stage 3 – Outline** — Student builds sentence-level outline from their own confirmed ideas. Coach supplies structure only.
- **Stage 4 – Sources** — Review and select the research leads. Build references as they go.
- **Stage 5 – Drafting** — Student writes every sentence from their outline. Coach offers example transition language and light cleanup flags only.
- **Stage 6 – Revise** — Citation audit, flow, voice check, export to Word/PDF.
- **AI Checker** (`/ai-checker`) — Standalone tool. Student pastes text, gets signal-strength scores (not "AI likelihood"). Free preview + email unlock for full report.
- **Blog** — Later. Not in first build.
- **Admin** — Passcode creation for free unlimited access (testing and Angie).

## 8. Design System — Editorial-Warm

**Direction:** Warm, minimal, scholastic. Think a quiet library or a well-kept notebook — calm, confident, never shouting. Inspired by Apple's restraint and Notion's warmth. Not dark-neon, not flat corporate, not busy.

### 8.1 Color Palette
- **Background (page):** `#FAF7F2` — warm off-white, like aged paper. Never pure white.
- **Background (cards/panels):** `#FFFFFF` — pure white, used only for elevated surfaces sitting on the warm page.
- **Text (primary):** `#1C2B2D` — deep ink, near-black with a slight green cast. High contrast, easy to read for long sessions.
- **Text (secondary):** `#5C6B6E` — muted slate for supporting labels, captions, helper text.
- **Text (tertiary/disabled):** `#9AA5A8` — light gray for placeholders and inactive states.
- **Accent (primary):** `#C4785A` — muted terracotta. Used ONLY for primary buttons, active states, and key highlights. One accent, used sparingly.
- **Accent (secondary):** `#7A9E7E` — soft sage. Used for success states, progress indicators, positive feedback moments.
- **Accent (warning):** `#D4A017` — warm amber. Used for logic-check warnings and the anonymous-user progress-loss banner.
- **Accent (error):** `#C0392B` — restrained red. Used only for true errors and the red-alert logic checkpoint.
- **Border/divider:** `#E8E2DA` — soft warm gray. 1px, low contrast.

### 8.2 Typography
- **Headlines:** `Fraunces` (Google Fonts, serif, variable weight 400–700). Scholastic, literary, warm. Use for page titles, stage names, and section headers.
- **Body:** `Source Sans 3` (Google Fonts, sans-serif). Humanist, highly legible at small sizes. Use for all body text, questions, and coaching prompts.
- **Mono/code:** `JetBrains Mono` — only for technical strings, code, or raw pasted content.
- **Scale:** 16px base. Headlines: 32px (h1), 24px (h2), 20px (h3). Body: 16px. Small/caption: 13px. Line height 1.6 for body, 1.2 for headlines.
- **Max content width:** 680px for reading text. Wider layouts (dashboards, cards) may use up to 1100px with a centered column.

### 8.3 Spacing
- Base unit: 8px. All spacing is multiples of 8.
- Page padding: 48px vertical, 24px horizontal (mobile), 64px horizontal (desktop).
- Section gaps: 64px between major sections on a page.
- Card padding: 32px.
- Element gaps within a card: 16px.
- Generous margins everywhere. If it feels cramped, double the space.

### 8.4 Layout & Components
- **One frame per screen.** A single question, prompt, or content block dominates. No competing elements.
- **Ghost skeleton:** A faint, non-interactive outline of the full paper floats in the background (low opacity, ~8%) during Drafting and later stages. Clicking it opens a review overlay; it never blocks the active frame.
- **Milestone pop-up:** When a stage section completes, a brief overlay shows the growing paper outline with clickable section chips. "Ready for [next stage]?" with a Review option and a Continue option.
- **Buttons:** Primary — terracotta background (`#C4785A`), white text, 8px border radius, soft shadow (`0 2px 8px rgba(196,120,90,0.25)`), 48px min height for touch. Secondary — white background, terracotta text and 1px border, same radius. Ghost — no border, terracotta text, underline on hover.
- **Inputs:** White background, 1px warm-gray border, 8px radius, 16px padding, 48px min height. Focus ring: 2px terracotta, offset 2px.
- **Cards:** White background, 1px warm-gray border (or no border with soft shadow `0 1px 4px rgba(28,43,45,0.06)`), 12px radius.
- **Progress indicator:** Thin sage bar or simple step counter. Never a busy multi-colored stepper. Inside a paper it is a slim horizontal row (see section 9), never a sidebar list.
- **Logic-check states:** No flag = quiet sage checkmark. Light feedback = amber note. Strong pushback = amber banner with the claim restated. Red alert = terracotta banner, continue-anyway is a secondary button, never hidden.
- **Anonymous banner:** Amber background, warm amber text, appears at top of demo sessions: "Progress won't be saved. Create a free account to save and resume anytime."

### 8.5 Copy Rules
- Short sentences. Plain words. Lead with benefit before feature.
- Headlines state the outcome, not the mechanism. "Your paper, your words, every step" not "AI-powered writing assistance platform."
- One idea per sentence. No jargon. No exclamation points in body copy.
- Button text is a verb + object: "Start free paper," "Upload assignment," "Confirm section."
- Coaching prompts are warm and specific, never clinical. "What pulled you to servant leadership?" not "Please elaborate on your choice."
- Error messages name the problem and the next step. Never blame the user.

### 8.6 Motion
- Minimal. 150–200ms transitions on hover and focus only.
- No autoplay, no parallax, no decorative animation that delays content.
- Page transitions: simple fade, 200ms.
- Skeleton loaders use the warm-gray shimmer, never spinners alone.

### 8.7 Accessibility
- WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Text contrast 4.5:1 against backgrounds.
- All interactive elements keyboard-navigable with visible focus rings.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Form labels always visible, never placeholder-only.

## 9. Pau Conversation Model (added Nov 22, 2026 per Colton)
Pau should feel like a present coach, closer to how Grok talks than a series of separate text messages.
- **One reply per turn.** Each Pau turn is ONE continuous message block (paragraphs inside it are fine; it may stream in). Never several stacked bubbles saying one thing. This includes the greeting + first question, the assignment read-back + confirmation question, an offer + its choices, and Pau's reply to a choice. Reloaded history renders the same way.
- **Natural replies, no "Fix something".** After the read-back the student just replies in the same chat input ("looks good", "do you need my due date?", "it's actually 1500 words, due Dec 3"). Pau classifies the reply (confirm, question, correction, extra info). Corrections update the extracted fields and Pau re-confirms in one reply. Pau never advances on an ambiguous reply. The student's own words are kept verbatim. Stage gates still apply.
- **Light progress, no stage sidebar inside a paper.** A slim horizontal indicator (stage markers + current stage name) replaces the six-stage list. It never pushes the input down on mobile or desktop.
- **Voice.** When Voice is on it works on mobile (unlocked by the student's tap), stays on, and reads only Pau's own words, never source text or the student's words.
- **Unclear replies get a real clarifying question.** "hmm maybe" is answered with a question that re-asks and offers a way forward ("No worries. Does anything in there look off, like the length, due date or sources? Or tell me “looks good” and we'll start."). It never advances.
- **Corrections overwrite everywhere.** A corrected field replaces the old value in the whole recap (a single "1,500 words" replaces a 1,200–1,500 range). Due dates are read back with a year (the next upcoming occurrence). Read-backs are full sentences ("This is for ENG 101 with Prof. Dana Ruiz: a …"), and the citation style stays specific (MLA 9, APA 7).
- **Offers fold into the current reply.** The hesitation offer, its choices and Pau's answer are a soft last paragraph inside the current question's bubble, never a second bubble.
- **Reloads are silent.** A reload never re-reads old bubbles; only a NEW Pau turn (after the student taps, types or speaks on this page load) is spoken.
- **Sources and examples.** Pau introduces them in her own voice, then shows clickable search links (articles, news, videos) in the same reply; never made-up articles. She never reads source content as if she were the source; voice reads only her intro. The research-leads card follows this pattern.
- **Same chat-style input in Stages 2 and 3.** Stance and Outline use the same question-at-top chat, composer (mic / send; "+" only where there is a file to add), slim progress row and call-mode voice/mute as Stage 1. One Pau turn at a time: teach cards, the mirror, the probe, research leads, the lock-in and the logic check are each one Pau reply with tap choices inside it. Stage 3 asks one outline section at a time; the box starts with the student's own Stance notes. The voice nudge (Stage 6) folds into her reply wherever it appears. Every gate and its exact criteria stay the same (thin notes, logic check, skipped sections block Sources, outline saved before advancing), the conversation is saved so a reload resumes it, and a typed reply is always the student's reply (classified first, never turned into a chip pick); a plain yes still stands in for the Yes choice.
- **Requests are not notes.** Everything the student types in Stages 2–3 is classified first (model, with a rule fallback): content for the paper, a request to the coach ("ask me some probing questions"), or a question to Pau ("what do you mean?"). Requests and questions get her answer inside the current reply and are never saved as a note, checked by the logic check or prefilled into the outline.
- **Taps are not the student's words.** A tapped choice or lead shows as a small "Picked: …" chip, never as a student bubble. Only what the student typed or said appears as their message.
- **Relevance first.** The logic check first asks whether the claim answers the prompt and names a mismatch ("This doesn't answer the question your prompt asks: …") before any other critique. "Continue anyway" is acknowledged, and the section stays flagged in Stage 3.
- **Barge-in.** On a voice call, tapping Send or "Talk now" while Pau is talking stops her and takes the student's input. Otherwise listening stays paused while she talks (echo protection).
- **Short Stage 1 (Colton t221u + t222u, Nov 25, 2026; PC-32).** (1) Gather the assignment, documents, layout, course material, passages and links. (2) The read-back says conversationally what the paper truly asks, only from what the materials state (never an invented grading criterion or an assumed due-date year), and asks if that sounds right. (3) "Have you already thought about what you want to write about? … if you've got nothing yet, that's totally fine." Keep it light, never an interview: acknowledge, summarize back for confirmation, note it, move on. If they want to expand: listen, acknowledge, summarize, note, until they say that's it for now (max 4 turns). No chains of probing questions; at most one light clarifying question, only when the idea is unclear (lib/stage1/starting-point.ts ideasPlan). (4) Their starting point (has_ideas / read_material / no_clue, with their idea verbatim) is saved in paper_context.startingPoint and Stage 2 opens from it. (5) Ghost script: once Stage 1 completes, a full reference paper that meets the instructor's requirements, including the word range, is written in the background from Pau's own research: one research-and-plan step (≤3 web searches) with per-section word budgets, then section by section with no searches, then up to four proportional rewrite passes that bring the total into the range (never stored outside it) and stored server-only, encrypted, in its own paper_autosaves row (server:ghost_script; no migration). It is hidden guidance for Stage 2–3 coaching only: never sent to the client (not in any API response, page prop, RSC payload, Past chat, export or log), never quoted or paraphrased; an output gate drops any coaching reply sharing a 5-word run or a near-copy sentence with it. Retired from Stage 1 for new papers: the material "where are you with it?" question, the style/decision pick and its why, the leader pick, the story-angle screen, the early take and the spark offer; the pick, leader and early take now come up in Stage 2's stance interview. Older non-north-star papers keep the old flow.
- **Stage 2 FIT CHECK (Colton t227u).** When a stance note names a style and a leader, Pau checks once per pair whether the leader has good, well-documented support for that style (a short table of well-documented pairs first, else one fast verdict using the ghost's research titles). Solid: one affirming line, then on. Weak or random: brief kind feedback, the concept re-grounded in plain words (labelled general background), 1-2 better-fitting examples from the table, and theirs to keep: never overruled, their note unchanged, nothing locked without them. Unsure: says so and labels it general background (R2). The fit lines never ask a question (R1: the step's own ask is the one question). `lib/stage2/fit-check.ts`, `POST /api/papers/[paperId]/coach/fit`. Fires (QA 4:57 PM) once on Stage 2 entry when the pair is already known from Stage 1 (ideas, or an older pick), inline right after the "Earlier you picked…" opener and before the Intro lesson, and again only when a later Stage 2 note introduces or changes the pair. Each result is kept server-side (`server:fit_checks`), so a reload shows the same lines without a new check. Stage 1 ideas: a terse concrete idea ("Elon Musk, servant leadership") is summarized back and confirmed, never drilled; a yes/no/confirm phrase is never stored as idea content (`isConfirmOnly`); no ideas yet gets "That's totally fine, we'll find one together in Stage 2." (no reflection, no praise); the wrap-up ends on the Start Stage 2 button, not a question.
- **Ghost gate tuning (PC-32 QA 5:15 PM).** The output gate blocks real leakage, not generic wording. Only distinctive ghost runs count: 5-word runs that also appear in the assignment, brief, readings/sources context, or the student's own messages and notes are excluded, as are stock coaching phrases, runs that are mostly stopwords, and runs made only of everyday topic words (styles, team, change, vision...). Blocks on any distinctive 8-word run, on 2+ distinct distinctive 5-word runs (one copied stretch counts once), or on a near-copy of one ghost sentence. On a block, coach/answer retries once asking for different, plain conversational words (no paper-like prose); the empty fallback is used only if the retry is blocked too. Logged as `[coach-answer] {ghostBlocked, gate:{rule,hits5,hits8}, regen:{ok,rule,hits5,hits8}}`. `lib/ghost/script.ts` `ghostLeakCheck`, `tests/unit/pc32-ghost-gate.test.ts`.
- **Ghost gate, own-leader facts (PC-32 QA 5:33 PM).** "How did Sam Walton show servant leadership?" got a vague answer: the first draft shared 8-word runs with the ghost's own Walton FACT sentences, so it was blocked, and the retry ("no paper-like prose") came back generic. Now the gate protects the ghost's ARGUMENT (thesis and claim sentences: because, therefore, most effective, proves...) with the 8-word / 2×5-word / near-copy rules; its plain fact sentences are only protected against being reproduced (a distinctive 12-word run, or a sentence taking ≥ 85% of ≥ 10 novel content words from one AND covering ≥ 70% of it). Runs made only of everyday topic words plus the student's own terms (their question and notes: leader, style) never count. The retry asks for concrete facts about the leader (paper_context or labelled general background, never invented), not thesis or paper sentences, and the hidden guidance says the same. Stage 1 idea acks paraphrase as a clean phrase ("Got it. So you're thinking about Sam Walton as an example of servant leadership."), never raw text spliced in (same rule as #76), and no "Ooh, I like that".
- **Content answers: readings vs background, one ask (PC-32 QA 5:51 PM).** A content answer has two separate parts: (a) what the readings actually say, only from the reading key points/text in paper_context, never a reading credited with a claim it doesn't make, never the student's leader tied to an author unless that reading names them, and skipped when no reading is relevant; (b) facts about the leader, always under "From general background (check before citing):". The model returns them apart (`paras` + `background`); a post-check (`shapeContentAnswer`) drops a sentence crediting an author whose reading text isn't in the context, drops one tying the leader to an author whose reading doesn't name them, and moves stray leader facts under the label. Every coach answer keeps exactly ONE question, last (`oneQuestion`: earlier questions dropped, a stacked "…, and which…?" keeps its first ask). Write-it-for-me requests for any part (intro, body, conclusion, thesis, paragraph, sentence; "just give me the intro paragraph") get the thesis-style decline ("That part's yours to write…") with one question and no recap, on both the intent path and coach/answer. No "Back to your …" / "When you're ready…" tag when her last line already asks. The Stage 2 recap names the leader from the Stage 1 idea when the decision only says "he", and styles read lowercase mid-sentence ("servant leadership").
- **Voice capture runs in the background (Colton t219u, Nov 25, 2026; PC-32).** The student's voice, tone and language are learned quietly from everything they write or say, in every stage (Stages 1–3 today: every Stage 1 answer, every Stage 2/3 note, answer and chat message in their own words; never pasted assignment text, taps, or Pau's words). It is never a Stage 1 task, question, gate, checklist item, progress meter or note: no "writing sample" step, no word minimum, no "I have less of your voice" line. Stage 1 is only assignment capture, the summary, and an initial direction. The existing style_profiles (per paper) and voice_profiles (per user) rows are refreshed from the accumulated answers after each Stage 1 answer, on Stage 1 complete, on Stage 2/3 session saves (throttled) and on each stage move; anything that used the old sample falls back to the accumulated answers. Older papers that already answered the retired voice questions keep them in their history. Coaching-only and R1–R5 are unchanged.
- **Voice turn-taking (Colton t194u, Nov 25; PC-29).** On a Grok Voice call: (1) her first ~1.5 s of every reply can't be cut, and after that only sustained talk (≥0.6 s of voiced sound across short word gaps, clearly louder than her echo) pauses her; the pause becomes a real interruption only when the transcript has real words, otherwise (noise, a cough, "um", an ASR "thank you") she carries on from the exact spot, and a noise-only turn never gets a reply. (2) A real interruption truncates her turn where the student stopped hearing it; she answers them first, then brings the unheard point back in a few words if it still matters. A dropped connection reconnects (resumption) and picks up the cut line. (3) One input channel at a time: while the student types (focused box with text or a recent keystroke; on the upload screen focus alone) the mic sends silence and the status reads "Typing — mic paused"; a spoken turn that ends while typing gets no spoken reply (its words still land in the box), and only one response is ever pending. (4) No repeats: a Variety rule and her last lines ride in her instructions, and an app-triggered line that repeats one she just said is dropped before it's heard. Tunables live in lib/voice/realtime/turns.ts (TURN_TUNING). (5) Stage 1 voice follows the screen (QA round 2): every on-screen prompt is said word for word by the app (force_message; the read-back card is the confirmation, so only its closing line is said; voice turned on mid-chat says the current prompt; the next prompt after a spoken answer is the app's copy), her own replies are short reactions, and any question of hers that isn't the on-screen one is cut before it's heard (her reaction still plays; xAI's copy is truncated to what was heard). When Stage 1 is done, voice says what it's waiting for and ends once the stance interview starts. (6) One source of Stage 1 prompts (QA round 3): the intake hands the call a prompt only once the on-screen line has held still (~0.9 s) with no brief call in flight (≤8 s) and its question is actually rendered on the page; until then the call is told there is no prompt (so no question of hers passes). The relay never says a prompt from a tool result, and a queued prompt the screen has replaced is dropped. Stage 1 ends only after the last decision save lands, and the Stage 2 opener waits (≤5 s) for the paper context, so it opens with "Earlier you picked …".
- **Logic tiers.** Severe ("Logic alert", "Continue anyway") is only for a claim that is off-topic (the model decides relevance first on every section; word overlap is the fallback) or contradicts itself. A personal example backing an opinion is normal: at most a gentle nudge ("One example is a great start; what else shows this?").
- **Append-only history.** Pau's answers to requests and questions are saved in the transcript, so they survive the next turn and a reload; after answering she gives a short follow-up instead of repeating the opening ask.
- **Quick lesson in Body only.** The lesson concept's Quick lesson appears in the Body, where the concept is applied; the Introduction and Conclusion don't carry a concept and go straight to the probe.
- **On-topic means the prompt's subject.** Specific details of the subject count as addressing the assignment (for a start-time prompt: 8:30, the bell, first period, starting later). When the relevance check says on-topic, the cruder word match never adds "doesn't yet speak to the assignment".
- **Sends are never dropped or doubled.** The box empties the moment a message is sent. A message sent while Pau is still replying is queued with a visible note and goes out on its own when she's done. One send is in flight at a time, and the same answer is never appended twice.

- **Coming back (Colton, Nov 22 3:46 PM).** On a reload or a return, every chat (Stages 1–3) restores its history silently, scrolled to the bottom; nothing already shown is regenerated or read again (a per-paper marker, saved per user).
